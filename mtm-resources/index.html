A short explanation

What is multiple team membership?

Multiple team membership means that one person works in two or more teams during the same period. This happens in project work, healthcare, education, public services, factories, logistics, consulting, and many other settings.

The arrangement can spread useful experience across an organisation. It can also create frequent switching, mixed priorities, and unclear expectations. A good diagnosis starts with the shape of the work, not with a universal limit on the number of teams.

For educators

Two cases ready for class

Each case includes a learner handout and a separate solution guide. The language is simple enough for students who have not studied multiple team membership before.

01

Status and team re-entry

Back from the Task Force

Maya returns to her regular team after a visible assignment elsewhere. Her formal role has not changed, but her position across the two teams is different. The class must decide how her leader can use her experience without turning the return into a status contest.

02

Work design and switching

Four Teams, Two Very Different Weeks

Ruben and Aisha each belong to four teams at a distribution centre. One has a clear home team and protected blocks. The other changes team context throughout the day. The class must redesign the work without assuming that one team count fits everyone.
