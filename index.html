Joost van de Brake

Research and teaching on modern teamwork.

I study how people work together within and across teams, and I use this research in university teaching, in executive education, and in partnerships with organisations. My research focuses in particular on multiple team membership, hybrid work, and the stress and strain of modern team arrangements.

My formal name is Hendrik J. van de Brake, and academic records also use the forms H. J. van de Brake and HJ van de Brake.

I work at the Faculty of Economics and Business of the University of Groningen, where I am Associate Professor and Research Director of the Department of HRM and Organizational Behavior.

I received my PhD in Economics and Business from the University of Groningen in 2019, and I completed my MSc in Sociology there in 2013.

For more than ten years I have carried out applied research with research institutes, hospitals, energy and infrastructure companies, and ministries.

I am also an associate editor of Group and Organization Management. In 2022 I received an NWO Veni grant for my research on multiple team membership.

I supervise PhD candidates and Master’s theses, and in November 2026 I completed the PhD Supervision Excellence programme of the University of Groningen.

Working with organisations

In partnerships and applied projects with organisations, I draw on research and on years of experience with this kind of work.

Diagnose what is going on. I conduct employee surveys and analyse large datasets to find out what causes problems with engagement, burnout, turnover, and team performance. I then explain the findings in plain terms to HR, project controllers, and top management.

Design and evaluate interventions. I help organisations design experiments, pilots, and longitudinal studies that test whether a new way of working makes a difference before the organisation introduces it more widely.

Train and coach leaders. I design workshops for team leaders, department heads, and HR professionals on the basis of research findings. I adapt each workshop to the participants’ own work so that they can apply the findings in practice.
